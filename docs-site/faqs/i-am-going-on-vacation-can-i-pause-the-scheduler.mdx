---
title: "I'm going on vacation. Can I pause the scheduler?"
---

You can take a break from Anki, but you cannot pause forgetting. Moving due dates
does not preserve your memory: some cards may need relearning when you return.
You do not need to change the schedule to take time off. The following options can
help you prepare and make catching up more manageable.

## Before Your Break

- **Reduce or stop new cards in advance.** Newly learned cards need frequent
  reviews. In [Deck Options](/manual/deck-options#new-cards/day), lower **New
  cards/day**, or set it to `0`, while continuing to review cards you have already
  learned. This reduces new work without hiding existing reviews.
- **Check which decks your change affects.** For a break lasting several days,
  use the **This deck** daily limit rather than **Today only**. Changing the
  **Preset** limit affects all decks using that preset unless they have their own
  overrides. If you study subdecks separately, check their limits too. See
  [Per-Deck Daily Limits](/manual/deck-options#per-deck-daily-limits).
- **If you have extra time, consider reviewing ahead once.** On the computer,
  select a deck, then **Custom Study > Review ahead**. This can move some reviews
  beyond your break, but recently learned cards may still be due during it.
  Repeatedly reviewing ahead is not a substitute for spacing reviews; see
  [Reviewing Ahead](/manual/filtered-decks#reviewing-ahead).

For recurring busy days, such as weekends, built-in
[Easy Days](/manual/deck-options#easy-days) can reduce the reviews scheduled on
selected weekdays. It works with both FSRS and SM-2. Changing Easy Days alone does
not move already scheduled reviews, and it does not guarantee a day with no
reviews. It is not a way to block out an entire vacation.

## During Your Break

If you have some time to study, keep new cards at `0` and review the material most
important to you. A short session is useful even if you cannot finish every due
card. If you cannot study at all, leave the cards as they are and resume when you
return.

You can use [Maximum reviews/day](/manual/deck-options#maximum-reviews/day) to limit
the number of review cards shown. This hides excess reviews for the day; it does
not clear the backlog or stop those cards becoming more overdue. A daily limit is
also not a time limit, since forgotten cards may need multiple reviews.

## When You Return

Keep new cards at `0` until the review workload is manageable. Choose an amount of
time you can sustain each day, allowing for both overdue cards and cards that
become due while you catch up. You do not have to finish the backlog in one sitting.

Answer according to what you remember now. Use
[Again](/manual/studying#answer-buttons) when you forget an answer; **Hard** means
you recalled it correctly with difficulty. You do not need to reset your cards
just because you missed reviews.

For a large backlog, [filtered decks for catching up](/manual/filtered-decks#catching-up)
let you separate recent due cards from older overdue cards and work through the
older group gradually. This is optional; you can also continue reviewing in your
regular decks. Restore any daily limits you changed when you are ready to resume
your usual routine.

## Optional Scheduling Tools

If you use FSRS, the [FSRS Helper add-on](https://github.com/open-spaced-repetition/fsrs4anki-helper#usage)
offers tools for specific dates and longer breaks, including **Schedule a Break**
and **Advance/Postpone**. These are add-on features, separate from Anki's built-in
Easy Days. Read the add-on's current instructions and requirements before using
them. Postponing reviews trades a lighter workload now for a greater chance of
forgetting later; it does not remove the need to review.

Anki also has **Cards > Set Due Date** in the Browse screen for moving selected
cards to earlier or later dates. Use it conservatively: moving dates is not the
same as reviewing the cards, and moving everything far into the future can lead
to more relearning. See [Set Due Date](/manual/browsing#cards) for its behavior and
options.
